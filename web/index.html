Research explorer · Chicago lead service lines

Who gets new pipes first?

Chicago has to dig up and replace roughly 412,000 lead or possibly-lead water service lines over about twenty years. Budgets and crews are limited, so the order of the work decides how long each neighborhood keeps drinking through lead, what the program costs, and whether the deadline is met. This page lets you explore the schedules computed in the research paper Algorithmic Equity and Capital Allocation.

Efficiency vs. equity frontier

Each point is a schedule. The horizontal axis is the Q5 wait gap: how many years earlier (negative) or later (positive) the most socially vulnerable fifth of lines are reached than the citywide average. The blue line forces the vulnerable to be served even earlier than the health-welfare optimum; the gray dashed line forces them later. Both cost more, so delaying the vulnerable is never efficient here.

What is observed and what is modeled

Observed public data

  • 2025 Chicago Department of Water Management service-line inventory, geocoded by Inside Climate News, Grist and WBEZ: 491,406 lines after cleaning, 412,433 needing action (lead, galvanized requiring replacement, or unknown/suspected lead), plus 2021–2025 replacements and refusals of access.
  • CDC/ATSDR Social Vulnerability Index 2022 (tract level).
  • American Community Survey 2019–2023: children, adults, renters, building size, income, language.
  • HOLC redlining grades (1930s), crosswalked to tracts.
  • CDPH childhood blood-lead screening by community area, 1999–2013 (used only to validate, not to schedule).
  • Reported costs: about $31k per line on average, $34k in block programs, $39k one-off (city figures as reported in 2026); EPA's $4,700 national estimate for comparison.
  • Community-area boundaries: City of Chicago Data Portal.

Modeled assumptions & estimates

  • Unknown materials are imputed with a spatially cross-validated classifier (AUC 0.90).
  • Exposure: how much a lead line raises blood lead (children +0.8 µg/dL, adults +0.2 µg/dL) is an assumption, varied in the uncertainty analysis.
  • Valuation follows EPA's 2024 LCRI Economic Analysis (value per IQ point, cardiovascular mortality functions, value of a statistical life).
  • Operations: crews of 15,000 rising to 24,000 lines a year; capital budgets of $450M rising $40M a year to $820M; a $1.5M mobilization charge per block program; at least 250 lines per block batch.
  • Corridors: lines are grouped into 160 k-means corridors of ~2,600 lines; the map rolls corridor schedules up to community areas.
  • Discount and financing rates (2/3/7%; SRF 1.5%, bonds 5%) and the equity penalty ($4,000 per vulnerability-weighted line-year) are policy assumptions.
  • Every schedule here is a model output, not a city plan.
